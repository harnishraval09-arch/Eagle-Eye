§ 1Brief Facts

The document was submitted by Inspector R. Naidu of the Telangana Cybercrime Unit in connection with Reference No. TG/CYB/2024/0042, relating to suspected use and distribution of a forged COVID-19 vaccination certificate.

The examination was requested to determine whether the submitted certificate is a genuine document issued through the Government of India's CoWIN portal (NIC), or whether it has been fabricated or tampered with. A genuine CoWIN certificate was provided by NIC as a reference specimen for comparison.

§ 3Examination Methods Conducted

(a) Visual examination under white light · (b) UV light at 365nm & 254nm · (c) Stereomicroscopy 10×–40× · (d) Paper GSM measurement · (e) Transmitted light watermark verification · (f) Ink spread & printing process analysis · (g) Ink age retention test · (h) Digital metadata extraction via ExifTool v12.6 · (i) PDF structural & XMP history analysis · (j) Digital signature certificate chain verification · (k) Seal impression dimensional comparison · (l) Comparison against genuine CoWIN reference specimen

§ 5 — Expert Opinion  ·  Forgery Confidence Score: 92% — HIGH CONFIDENCE FORGERY

Based on the examination conducted, it is my opinion that the submitted COVID Vaccination Certificate bearing Reference No. TG/CYB/2024/0042 shows 18 anomalies across all 6 examination categories as detailed in the Findings section. The cumulative forgery confidence score is 92.0%, placing it firmly in the "High Confidence Forgery" classification range.

The document is, in all probability, a fabricated instrument and is NOT consistent with certificates issued by the Government of India through the CoWIN portal operated by NIC. The most conclusive indicators are: (1) PDF Creator metadata identifying Adobe Acrobat 2021 instead of the NIC CoWIN backend; (2) three post-creation edit events in the XMP modification history; (3) an invalid self-signed digital signature with an incomplete ByteRange; (4) signature tracing evidence confirmed by pen lift anomalies and complete absence of natural speed tremor; and (5) ink age testing establishing printing within 30 days, contradicting a claimed vaccination date 14 months prior.
