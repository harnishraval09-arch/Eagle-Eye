# Indian Evidence Act & Document Forgery Law

## Section 45 — Opinions of Experts (Indian Evidence Act, 1872)

> "When the Court has to form an opinion upon a point of foreign law or of science or art,
> or as to identity of handwriting or finger impressions, the opinions upon that point of
> persons specially skilled in such foreign law, science or art, or in questions as to
> identity of handwriting or finger impressions are relevant facts."

### Who Qualifies as an Expert
- Questioned Documents (QD) Examiner
- Forensic Science Laboratory (FSL) Officer
- Handwriting Expert certified by a recognized body
- Digital Forensics Examiner (for metadata/digital evidence)

### Weight of Expert Opinion
- Expert opinion is **relevant** but **not conclusive**
- It is one piece of evidence — courts weigh it alongside other evidence
- A well-reasoned, methodologically documented report carries significantly more weight
- Cross-examination on methodology is common — be precise and reproducible

## Admissibility Requirements

For a QD expert report to be admissible under Section 45:

1. **Qualifications stated**: Name, designation, years of experience, certifications
2. **Method documented**: Every examination step must be described
3. **Reproducibility**: Another expert should be able to repeat the examination
4. **Observation vs Inference separation**: Facts observed ≠ conclusions drawn
5. **No conjecture**: Every opinion must be grounded in observable evidence
6. **Signed declaration**: Examiner must sign with date and place

## Relevant IPC Sections for Document Forgery

| IPC Section | Offence | Punishment |
|-------------|---------|------------|
| Section 463 | Making a false document (forgery definition) | — |
| Section 464 | Making a false document — execution | — |
| Section 465 | Punishment for forgery | Up to 2 years + fine |
| Section 466 | Forgery of court records or public registers | Up to 7 years + fine |
| Section 467 | Forgery of valuable security, will, etc. | Up to 10 years / Life |
| Section 468 | Forgery for purpose of cheating | Up to 7 years + fine |
| Section 469 | Forgery for purpose of harming reputation | Up to 3 years + fine |
| Section 470 | Forged document (definition) | — |
| Section 471 | Using as genuine a forged document | Same as forgery offence |
| Section 420 | Cheating and dishonestly inducing delivery | Up to 7 years + fine |

## Expert Declaration Template

Use this exact format at the end of every report:

```
EXPERT DECLARATION

I, [Full Name], [Designation], [Organization/Department],
having [X] years of experience in Questioned Documents examination,
hereby solemnly declare that:

1. I have personally examined the document(s) listed in Section 2 of this report.
2. The examination was conducted using standard forensic methods as described in Section 3.
3. The findings and opinions expressed in this report are based solely on my scientific
   examination and professional expertise.
4. I have no personal interest in the outcome of this case.
5. This report is prepared for submission as expert evidence under Section 45 of the
   Indian Evidence Act, 1872.
6. The contents of this report are true to the best of my knowledge and belief.

Signature : _______________________
Name      : [Full Name]
Designation: [Designation]
Organization: [Organization]
Date      : [DD/MM/YYYY]
Place     : [City]
```

## Case Law References

- **Shashi Kumar Banerjee v. Subodh Kumar Banerjee (AIR 1964 SC 529)**:
  SC held that expert handwriting evidence must be corroborated; not conclusive alone.

- **Mobarik Ali Ahmed v. State of Bombay (AIR 1957 SC 857)**:
  Expert opinion on handwriting — court must independently assess the comparison.

- **State (Delhi Admin.) v. Pali Ram (1979 AIR 14 SC)**:
  QD examiner's testimony accepted as primary evidence in document fraud case.
